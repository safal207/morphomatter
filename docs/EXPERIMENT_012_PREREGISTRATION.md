# Experiment 012 — Geometry-Shaped Transition Landscape preregistration

## Question

Can chamber geometry shift the **external** conditions at which the already-frozen MorphoMatter transition law crosses its nucleation and frontier-onset boundaries, when the material law itself is unchanged?

This is a synthetic software experiment about **boundary conditions / transport geometry**, not a claim that pyramids or any macroscopic shape produce unknown physical fields.

## Frozen base

Experiment 012 is stacked on the frozen Experiment 011 head:

`30d2a0c9d29ce4013f0f83b78c068cb88226c5e2`

The underlying local transition law, Experiment 011 lambda interpolation, and local phase semantics remain unchanged.

## Geometry-only intervention

The experiment does **not** add a geometry bonus directly to transition probability.

Instead, each chamber shape is converted into two deterministic geometry-derived transport proxies:

1. **retention index** — `1 + mean Manhattan distance from active cells to the nearest chamber wall`;
2. **connectivity index** — mean 4-neighbor active degree divided by 4.

The slab geometry is the fixed reference.

For a geometry `g`:

`drive_gain(g) = retention_index(g) / retention_index(slab)`

`degree_ratio(g) = connectivity_index(g) / connectivity_index(slab)`

`coupling_gain(g) = sqrt(drive_gain(g) * degree_ratio(g))`

The same external condition is mapped to the unchanged local material law as:

`effective_drive = external_drive * drive_gain(g)`

`effective_coupling = external_coupling * coupling_gain(g)`

`effective_threshold_scale = external_threshold_scale`

No site, damage pattern, random seed, or target label is supplied to the adapter.

## Frozen geometry masks

All masks live on a `21 × 21` grid, are required to be 4-neighbor connected, and must remain within ±6% active-cell area of the `225`-cell slab reference.

The five frozen shapes are:

1. `slab` — centered `15 × 15` square (`225` cells);
2. `cylinder_like` — centered disk cross-section with radius `8.5` cells;
3. `pyramid_like` — 2D triangular cross-section, apex at top center and full-width base at the bottom;
4. `concave_hourglass` — symmetric wide–narrow–wide chamber;
5. `meandering_channel` — connected 11-cell-wide channel whose centerline shifts laterally through the chamber.

The names are geometric labels only. In particular, `pyramid_like` is **not** a model of the Moscow/Golod pyramid and carries no special physical coefficient.

## Frozen transition slice

Experiment 012 uses the Experiment 011 reference threshold slice:

- `lambda = (0.00, 0.20, 0.40, 0.60, 0.80, 1.00)`;
- `threshold_scale = 0.80`;
- frontier ordered-neighbor support `q = 0.25`;
- external frontier propagation drive `0.12`.

The two mapped surfaces are:

- `G-C1`: external drive needed for isolated nucleation onset;
- `G-C2`: external coupling needed for frontier-growth onset at `q = 0.25`.

Experiment 011 `C3` commit is not the primary geometry endpoint because its zero crossing stayed below the tested coupling envelope across the frozen map.

## Analytic roots

For the interpolated material config `cfg` and geometry gains `gd`, `gc`:

`G-C1 = (cfg.barrier * 0.80 - cfg.spontaneous_rate) / (cfg.nucleation_drive_gain * gd)`

and

`G-C2 = (cfg.barrier * 0.80 - cfg.frontier_base - cfg.frontier_drive_gain * (0.12 * gd)) / (cfg.frontier_neighbor_gain * 0.25 * gc)`

provided the relevant denominator is positive.

## Independent numerical scan

The analytic roots must be independently checked by calling the actual frozen `NucleationLattice._transition_probability` through the geometry adapter.

Numerical measurement ranges:

- external drive: `0.02 → 1.20`, step `0.01`;
- external coupling: `0.00 → 2.50`, step `0.01`.

The pre-existing control envelope used for reachability classification remains:

- external drive `[0.02, 0.80]`;
- external coupling `[0.00, 1.80]`.

A numerical root is consistent when it is within one scan step (`0.01`, plus floating tolerance) above the analytic zero crossing, matching the first strictly-positive probability point.

## Primary result rule

Protocol/implementation inconsistency is a technical failure and should fail CI.

If every preregistered point is consistent, scientific interpretation is frozen as:

- `GEOMETRY_SHIFTS_TRANSITION_SURFACES` if at `lambda = 1.00` at least two non-slab geometries shift either `G-C1` or `G-C2` by `>= 0.05` absolute external-control units versus slab;
- otherwise `GEOMETRY_EFFECT_SMALL_IN_SURROGATE`.

Secondary descriptive output reports whether a geometry moves a root into or out of the frozen external control envelope relative to slab. This does not change the primary label.

## Non-claims

Experiment 012 does not establish:

- a physical pyramid effect;
- anomalous fields generated by geometry;
- a real temperature, pressure, magnetic, acoustic, or electric phase diagram;
- physical nucleation shifts in water or any real material;
- physical energy savings;
- causality beyond the declared synthetic transport adapter.

The supported question is narrower: **inside a preregistered geometry-coupled software surrogate, does changing chamber shape alter the external control values needed to cross the same unchanged local transition surfaces?**
